## The question Max asked after the meeting: too structured, or a model that doesn't know what to look for?

Max, 2026-10-04: "in theory … if we're running Opus … it should be able to look
at the drawings. And as long as it's not being kind of narrowed down and
bottlenecked, it should just be able to have an understanding of … which each
measurements are."

**What runs** (read from `origin/staging`, not assumed):

- **Model**: `claude-opus-5`, effort high, adaptive thinking, 128k output
  (`anthropic.ts:45`, since `2cce414`, 2026-09-23). Not Opus 5.5, because 5.5
  refuses a forced `tool_choice`.
- **How the drawing arrives**: the PDF goes whole, as a native `document` block.
  The app does not rasterise it, set a resolution, or extract text.
- **What it is asked to do**: about 2.5k tokens of prompt and 1.4k words of tool
  schema. Every figure on the page (`"Every dimension figure on the page"`), each
  with **seven required fields**, up to 120 per item. For each figure it must
  also say whether it is an overall W / D / H / SH / Dia, with evidence.

**The read is both, and neither half is "Opus can't read drawings".**

1. **The task is a transcription with the real question buried in it.** Picking
   the three overall figures of a desk is the thing anybody wants. The model is
   asked it 68 times over, once per figure, inside a job of copying every
   radius and reveal on an A1 sheet. Most of the 68 rows are cost with no
   value: 57 were folded away unread, and the one that mattered (SECTION B) was
   in the fold.
2. **The guidance on views is one sentence, and it is silent on exactly what
   this sheet has.** `extraction-schema.ts:506` covers front, side and plan
   views. It says nothing about **sections**, numbered elevations, a plan whose
   top figure is not the outside (a curved desk), or **one figure per slot**.
   The model put SECTION A into Depth and two figures into Width. Neither
   contradicts what it was told.
3. **The page may be too dense at the resolution the model sees it.** This is a
   **hypothesis, and the model raised it itself**: "Many small fractional
   dimension strings are dense and some may have been read from layout
   proximity". A whole A1 sheet in one document block means 1/8"-high fractions
   at a fraction of their printed size. The obvious test: send the sheet's views
   as high-resolution crops beside the whole page. The app already knows the
   view regions and crops them in the browser. Measure before believing it.

So: **narrow the question** (the overall figures, with the view each came from,
asked first and on its own), **widen the eyes** (crops at full resolution), and
**let a person point at a found figure** (D below). Before changing anything,
measure on this pack. It has 29 distinct one-page drawings. A golden of their
overall W / D / H, written by a person off the PDFs, is an afternoon's work, and
`npm run measure:drawings` already reads staged runs.

This revisits the 2026-09-18 thesis ("code was inferring what the model could
have been asked to read off the page") and does not reverse it. That change
moved the slot decision to the model. This one asks the model a clearer question
and gives it a better picture.
