### 3.31 Substrate — a genuinely new concept (1:22:14–1:23:38)

> *"One of the very frequent TGQ / TG1 and TG0 differentiations we have is that
> the client will say, oh, we want it in oak. They don't know exactly what
> colour it is yet. So we will record that they want it in oak, and we can price
> it in oak, and we're pricing it in oak with a stain and a lacquer, regardless
> of what the stain is going to be."*

> *"In Villiers we just free-text type that, and have it as — we call it
> substrate. It's not a great word, but we'll say oak substrate. So everybody
> knows we're building this in oak regardless of what the finish is going to be.

> *"It'd be good to be able to have that as an option for finishes."*

**This is not a field rename. It is a second, coarser, separately-settled layer
under a finish, and it is what makes an item quotable while its finish is
TBC.** Nothing in the app models it.

## 4. What this changes in the repo

Each entry says what is written down today, what the meeting changed, and how
big the change is. **Nothing here is built.**

### 4.1 `run` → `phase` (D1) — mechanical, wide, do it early

`spec_runs` keeps its name in the schema; **the word on every screen and in
every document becomes "phase"**. Touches `SpecTable`, the project tabs, the
BOQ review, the drawings review, the chase screen, the export's own headings,
`docs/`, and `CLAUDE.md` / `AGENTS.md`.

Do it in one pass, not gradually — a half-renamed vocabulary is worse than
either end of it, because "run" and "phase" would then look like two things.

### 4.2 Levels stop constraining, and the matrix carries the gate

This is the sharpest tension with what is written down.

**Today:** `questionTier` refuses a null level and a record with no level is a
blocker on the drafts screen. That is the *fallback* half of TGQ
(`requirements.tgq_levels`, all three levels on all 728 rows). Matthew's own
matrix half (`spec_field_gates`, 0026) **has no level column at all** and
already answers without one.

**What Matthew asked for:** the level must never cause a specification field to
be unavailable. Sebastian's shape — show everything, demote and grey what the
level says is unlikely — is the agreed design.

**What this means concretely:**

- The level keeps two jobs it already has and both are safe: it picks the BWS
  boilerplate for the quote, and it is a human decision recorded on the record.
- The level must not gate what can be *recorded*. Check first whether anything
  actually hides a question by level today; if the checklist already shows
  everything, this is a presentation change (sort + grey), not a model change,
  and it is much smaller than the conversation made it sound.
- The fallback TGQ half is the only place a level currently *blocks*, and the
  fix for that is not to weaken the level — it is to get the rest of Matthew's
  matrix written (§5, the cabinetry half). Until then a level-less record on an
  uncovered category still cannot be tiered, and the screen should say **why**
  rather than demanding a level.

**Do not remove `spec_records.level`.** Matthew did not ask for that, it feeds
the boilerplate, and 0025 already keeps the guess (`level_suggested`) out of
every gate's reach.

### 4.3 Dimensions: keep the slots, add the qualifier (§3.23)

`composeDimensionCell` and the five slots are **endorsed**. What is missing is a
person-typed qualifier that travels with the dimensions and survives to the
file, for `1250 (L-shaped return)` and `footstool`.

0029 already added `record_attributes.qualifier` for the placement line, and
`EXPORT_QUALIFIER_MODE` joins it inline. **The composed DIMENSIONS cell
deliberately carries no qualifier** — because four slots off three pages could
carry four placements and picking one would invent a fact. Matthew's request is
different: **one qualifier for the dimension statement as a whole**, authored by
a person, not read off a page. That is a new thing and it needs its own home;
it must not reuse the per-attribute qualifier.

### 4.4 The chase email is grouped by question × area; the chase screen stays per item

**The screen and the email are different artefacts and the meeting settled them
differently.**

- The **screen** is a list of furniture lines, collapsed, which is what Max
  asked for on 2026-09-17 after seeing 823 rows. Matthew used it without
  complaint and clicked into items happily. **Leave it.**
- The **email** must not repeat one question across ten lines. Group by the
  question, then by area: *"for the dressing area, we don't have a metalwork
  finish"*. The table format itself is fine — Matthew called it *"neat"* — so
  this is a regrouping, not a rewrite.

This is a change to `chase-template.ts` and the coverage rows behind it, not to
`chase-grouping.ts`. The guarantee that the body and the coverage are the same
set must survive the regrouping — that is what the send gate rests on.

**Area needs to become a real filter**, on the chase screen and the spec table.
It is the BOQ's fourth column and is currently a free-text search.

**And the recipient may be internal** (§3.24) — the CAM, sales, production. The
draft path assumes a client-facing chase today; nothing breaks, but the wording
and the tier banner were written for a client.

### 4.5 A "fill it in yourself" screen (§3.24)

Max's own proposal, accepted: the chase inventory with an **edit box instead of
a tick box**. It is the missing step between "intake reviewed" and "draft a
chase", and it is the screen Matthew wants open in a client handover meeting.

It writes checklist answers, so `source_kind = 'manual'` and every answer is out
of reach of `applyAnswerFills` afterwards — which is already the rule.

**The roles it has to serve are named** (§3.24): the PM loads and reviews, the
CAM fills in what they remember, the client answers what is left. That is at
least two people on one project and it is an argument for it being a screen
rather than a mode of the chase screen.

Sebastian's link-back-into-the-app variant is **not decided** and should not be
built on spec: it implies external logins and a client-facing surface, which is
a security and scope decision nobody took.

### 4.6 An outstanding-by-question view (§3.26)

*"Show me all the jobs with dimensions missing."* Same data as the chase
inventory, pivoted. `loadOutstanding` already produces the rows; this is a
second grouping of one loader, not a second loader — which is the rule that
keeps two screens from disagreeing about TGQ.

### 4.7 Substrate (§3.31)

New. A finish can carry a **substrate** — `oak` — that is settled and quotable
while the finish itself is TBC. It is the thing that lets an item pass TGQ
without the stain being chosen.

Open questions before this can be designed, all for Matthew:

1. Is substrate a property of the **project finish** (the library row) or of the
   **item's attribute**? The oak is a fact about `WD-05`; the placement is a fact
   about the item.
2. Does a known substrate make a TBC finish **satisfy** TGQ, or merely reduce
   it? This changes gate arithmetic and must not be guessed.
3. Does it reach BWS as its own column, as part of the value, or not at all?

### 4.8 Palettes are now obtainable (§3.32) — the biggest single unlock

`CLAUDE.md` records five palettes seeded with **zero options** because BWS owns
them and this app has never had them: timber finish, metal finish, seat build,
back cushion, stud. The honest state was right, and the meeting ended it —
Matthew showed the exact screen they live on.

**How to take them, and the traps:**

- Source is the **Palette options** box on
  `bws.whistlercloud.com/standard_specification_fields/<id>/edit`, per field
  where `Field type` is `palette`. The field index marks which those are.
- **It is a scrape, not an export.** Matthew: *"I don't know how to get all of
  the options out on a download."* Max has scraped BWS boilerplates before, so
  the method exists. BWS production is fine to read from.
- **Never seed from a field's "Values" page.** That is what people have typed,
  including `self-piped` thirteen times — and somebody on this call had already
  drawn a wrong conclusion about the stud field from exactly that page.
- Stud options carry a **BWE Code** (`U1660-6031`). Keep it — it is BWS's own
  identifier and the thing that makes an option checkable.
- The `external-vocabulary-sync` skill is the procedure. `synced_at` stops being
  null, and the db-tier test asserting those five are empty has to be replaced
  with one asserting they are populated and unchanged since the sync.
- BWS stays **read-only**: this is a read, and the org rule that nothing writes
  to BWS is untouched.

**Blocked on: Max having a real BWS account** (§3.32). Nothing else here can
start until that exists.

Once seeded, `normalisePaletteValue` and `AnswerValue` already do the rest, and
the intake gains what Matthew asked for: match to the palette, flag it, offer
the dropdown, keep free text.

### 4.9 Product code, and the two counts beside it (§3.20, §3.21)

Product code is `unanswerable` on every record with a matrix view, which under
the gate chain means **no record in the sandbox can satisfy TGQ, and therefore
none can reach TG0 or TG1**. That is correct behaviour and must not be fixed by
weakening the chain. It needs one decision from Matthew and then a seed or a
migration.

Beside it sits a smaller but more immediate problem: **the gate panel said "5 to
answer" and the button said "Chase the 4", and nobody on the call could tell
why.** The reason — Spec notes is a manual entry and is not chased — is right,
and the screen says none of it. Two numbers differing by one, side by side, on
the screen whose whole job is to report what is outstanding.

### 4.10 A confirmed record has to be editable, and findable (§3.22)

Matthew went looking for confirm/update on an already-confirmed record and did
not find it. The repo's rule is deliberate — *"an applied proposal is immutable
history, and undoing an answer is something a person does on the record screen,
on purpose"* — and the route to doing it on purpose is what is missing.

This is the same failure as §3.19: the capability exists and the way in does
not. **Do not relax the immutability; surface the path.**

### 4.11 Versioning becomes two-part (D2)

`record_snapshots.snapshot_no` is a single integer per record. The agreed scheme
is `<issue>.<internal>`: internal edits bump the minor; issuing to the client
bumps the major and resets the minor.

Four things to get right:

- **An issue is an event, not a number.** Something has to record *"this went to
  the client on this date"* — which is what a named baseline already is
  (`baseline_members`, 0013, materialised under the project lock). The issue
  number should be derived from the baselines, not stored twice.
- **Does the client-facing version live on the record or the project?** Tony's
  framing (*"they'll be on V3"*) is project- or issue-level; the per-record
  diff is the internal one. Both were discussed as though they were one thing.
- **Does sign-off bump the major as well as issuing?** Tony said *"when it's
  signed off by the client it's V4"*, which is a second increment on top of the
  issue. Unresolved in the room.
- **V0 or V1 first?** Matthew's *"it starts off as version zero"* and
  Sebastian's *"all the internal ones is V1, 1.1, 1.2"* are different starting
  points.

The internal minor is **not secret**: Tony's example is telling the client
outright that V3.15 means unissued changes since V3.

### 4.12 The BWS export target is about to change (§3.35)

`CLAUDE.md` says the export is a **complete dataset** a human uploads, never a
delta, carrying no `Id` and no `Job Number`, and that a partial export silently
wipes every field it omits. That was written against Tim's existing CSV grid
importer.

**Matthew has asked Tim for a different importer** — *"specs plus free text and
a couple of other bits"*. Three things follow and none of them can be assumed:

1. **Is the new importer replace or merge?** The whole complete-dataset rule
   exists because the current one replaces. If Tim's is a merge, the rule
   changes; if it replaces, it stays. **Do not change the export until this is
   answered in writing.**
2. **Images by public URL.** Matthew: *"the images need to be in a public
   website."* This app's blobs are **private by design**, addressed by pathname,
   scoped to `projects/<id>/`, and never fetched from a client-supplied URL.
   Serving item pictures from a public URL is a real security decision about
   NDA-covered client material, not a configuration change. It needs its own
   discussion.
3. The 27 BWS-owned job columns and the `Name`/`Client Code` judgements in
   `docs/plans/questions-for-matthew.md` should be settled **in the same
   conversation as Tim's column contract**, not separately.

### 4.13 A stable build for Matthew (§3.34)

Today `staging` is both "what Max pushes to hourly" and "what Matthew is using".
Tony is right that those cannot be the same thing during D4.

The cheapest honest version: a second Vercel deployment pinned to a tagged
commit, **with its own database** (Sebastian's point about a clean data set),
that Max promotes to deliberately. `APP_ENV` / `DATABASE_ENVIRONMENT` already
refuse to start when they disagree, and `docs/environments.md` already carries
the two naming traps — this is a third environment inside that model, not a new
model.

**Do not call it production.** No production branch, and `house/deployment.md`
still governs.

### 4.14 Loading the finishes library from a schedule (§3.15)

Matthew asked for exactly what `project_finishes` is, and named the document
that fills it. `finishes_schedule` has been a `document_kind` since 0007 with a
prompt and no usable output shape: `RawProposal` carries `attributeRaw` and
`valueRaw` and **no field for a finish code**.

The fix is a tool-schema change, and `CLAUDE.md` already records its cost: it
**forces a re-read of every document already read** — eleven billed calls for
the Panther pack alone, against a Claude usage limit Max has already come close
to (§1). That cost is now clearly worth paying, but it is a decision with a
number attached and it should be taken deliberately, once, at the same time as
any other schema change to the drawings tool.

### 4.15 The intake needs a reference of what normal looks like (§3.36)

Max's ask — *"out of the 3000 sofas we've made, the majority fall between these
dimensions"* — is a small, cheap, high-value document, and it is the only thing
this app needs from the knowledge-base thread in the short term. It would give
the unit-resolution model a fourth signal that is not a project default.

**It is not a licence to infer.** The rule that a unit resolves in one fixed
order and stops, and that nothing is filled from a sister item or a category
template, is what M8 exists to prove. A typical-dimensions reference is a
**flag** ("this sofa would be 8m wide"), never a value.

### 4.16 What the knowledge base would eventually change here (§3.36)

Not next, and worth recording so it is not rediscovered. Tony's proposal, if
built, would give this app: cited sources for anything the intake suggests; a
per-client profile of what a client has liked before; and standards surfaced at
spec time, including the ones worth selling (sprung front edges) and the ones
worth flagging before somebody breaks them by accident (glued-and-screwed legs
on a bun foot). Every one of those is a **suggestion with evidence**, which is
the shape the app already uses for a guessed slot or a suggested finish kind.
