# The TGQ checklist — what Matthew is being asked for

**Raised 2026-09-16 by Max, after the review with Matthew. The workbook is
generated by [`tools/tgq-checklist.mjs`](../../tools/tgq-checklist.mjs);
regenerate it rather than hand-editing the file.**

## What TGQ is

**TGQ is the pre-sale gate: we know enough to put a price on this item.** Not
enough to build it — enough to quote it. Everything else can still be TBC.

It is the milestone the app is being built towards. Today the completion view
reports confirmed / TBC / missing across all 728 cheat-sheet questions, which
tells a KAM how full the form is and not whether they can send a quote. Those
are different questions, and only the second one is worth anything at tender
stage.

TGQ sits below the handover's TG0 / TG1 / TG2. Those are unauthored too —
`requirements.required_at_gate` is null on all 728 rows and there is no `gates`
table — and they stay out of scope. TGQ first, because it is the one the
pre-sale product needs.

## The three levels

Matthew described a three-level system per category: **simple sofa, complex
sofa, hero sofa** — or **simple table, table with metalwork, hero table**. What
changes between them is how much has to be known before a price is possible. A
hero item has intricate stitching to price; a table with metalwork has metal to
price; a simple one has neither.

This is not an invention of this repo's. **BWS already uses that vocabulary**:
the 45 boilerplate product codes captured on 2026-09-14 come in
`, Simple` / `, with Metalwork` pairs across the upholstery categories, and
`Hero` variants across cabinetry. The workbook shows each category's existing
BWS variants beside the level names so Matthew can correct the names to what
the business actually says.

Nothing is modelled in the database yet. The level is a property of the ITEM,
not of the category, so once the levels are named there is a second question to
answer — who sets an item's level, and when — and that is deliberately not
being asked in the same sitting.

## What the workbook asks

One question per row, four possible answers per level:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Yes** | we cannot quote this level without it |
| **No** | we can quote without it; it is still needed later |
| **N/A** | this question does not belong on this category's sheet at all |
| **?** | unsure, or worth a conversation |

Blank means not looked at yet, which is why blank and **No** are different —
the same distinction the app already makes between `missing` and an answer.

**N/A earns its place.** The cheat sheets are shared between categories and
several questions arrived on sheets they do not belong on: the Consoles sheet
asks for `Stitching spec` and `Stud spec`, the Dining tables sheet asks whether
the item is fully outdoor. Ticking N/A prunes the matrix at the same time, for
free, by the only person who can.

## The one editorial decision in it

**17 questions are hoisted onto their own sheet.** The entire
"Project / commercial" block — TOE agreement, expedited confirmation, sales
folder, sign-off visit, contact list, prototype, project context, floor plans,
access check, access OK, delivery route, counter samples, special finish, FSC,
deposit, BWS project page, COM payment plan — is **byte-identical across all 17
cheat sheets**. The generator asserts that rather than assuming it, and refuses
to build the workbook if a future revision breaks it.

Asking Matthew the same seventeen questions seventeen times is ceremony, not
consent. They are on the "Every category" sheet, answered once, and they still
carry the three level ticks. The workbook says so on its first sheet and asks
him to flag in the Notes if one of them really does vary by category.

The saving is the difference between **2,184 and 1,368 tick decisions**.

## What is NOT being asked here

**The BWS field mapping.** Each row shows which BWS column its answer would
land in, greyed, for context only. That mapping is this repo's judgement and
reviewing it is the separate four-question note —
[`questions-for-matthew.md`](questions-for-matthew.md). Mixing the two would
turn a tick-through into an audit.

**TG0 / TG1 / TG2.** Out of scope until the Panther export is judged correct.

## What comes back, and what happens to it

The `Key` column on every row is `<category slug>:<sort_order>` — exactly the
pair `db/seed/0003_requirements.sql` keys on, so a row's identity survives
Matthew sorting, filtering or re-ordering the sheet. The hoisted rows carry
`ALL:<sort_order>` and expand back across all 17 categories on the way in.

Turning the returned workbook into behaviour is **a re-seed plus a migration,
and no application logic moves** — the same rule as every other cheat-sheet
revision. Concretely:

1. a migration adding the TGQ level dimension to `requirements` (three
   nullable booleans, or a `requirement_levels` child table — decide when the
   answers are in and the shape is known, not before);
2. a re-seed carrying Matthew's ticks;
3. the completion view reading TGQ instead of, or alongside, the flat count.

Until then the workbook is the record and nothing in the app changes.

## The known gap

**An item's level is not recorded anywhere.** The workbook produces a rule of
the form "for a hero sofa, dimensions are required to quote", and the app
currently has no way to know that a given BOQ line is a hero sofa. Setting a
level will be a person's decision on the record, alongside category — it is not
derivable from a BOQ line, and guessing it would put the whole gate on a guess.
That is a known next step, not an oversight.

## Regenerating it

```bash
node tools/tgq-checklist.mjs
```

Writes `out/TGQ-checklist-<date>.xlsx` (gitignored — the output is a working
document, the generator is the artefact worth keeping). `--out <path>` puts it
elsewhere. It reads the seed files, not the database, so it needs no
`DATABASE_URL` and cannot be affected by the state of any environment.
