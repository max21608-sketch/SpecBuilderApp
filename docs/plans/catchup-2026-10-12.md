## Checked afterwards: one item, two TGQ numbers

Matthew read **"44 missing for the second"** off the phase table at 37:40. He
read **"those draws with the 20 missing TGQs"** off the fill-in screen at
1:00:02. Same record, `GR-FUR-26 Drawers`, same session. Nobody noticed.

**Checked in the source, and it is real.** The two screens count TGQ by
different rules:

- the phase table (`/api/records`, `to_quote_outstanding`) counts every
  outstanding question whose tier is `to_quote`, readiness questions included;
- the fill-in and chase screens (`countOutstanding`,
  `src/lib/chase-grouping.ts:256`) count only `to_quote` questions of kind
  `spec_field`, and put readiness questions under "also outstanding".

For a category on Matthew's matrix the record screen explains the gap in words
(`src/lib/chase-counts.ts`, "6 to answer at TGQ · 4 to chase · 2 you record on
this item's details", on screen at 19:20). For a category on the placeholder it
does not. `Side Table @ Bathroom` reads **47 TGQ** on its quote-readiness tile
beside a **Chase the 20** button, with no sentence between them (on screen at
7:00 and 1:40). **The 27 between them are mostly the project-wide questions
from the section above.**

This is the "TGQ is one name over two models" failure again, in a new place.
Each rule is defensible on its own screen. Two different numbers under one
name is not. The obvious fix is one predicate for both screens. Which predicate
depends on the project-level question above:

- **Project-wide questions never count per item.** They are asked once,
  per project. Item-level readiness questions keep counting where the tier says
  so. The phase table would drop to roughly the fill-in screen's figure.
- **Or keep counting readiness questions everywhere**, and make the fill-in and
  chase screens say why their number is smaller.

`countOutstanding` excluding every readiness question has a second, smaller
problem. On a matrix category, the readiness questions with a `local_key`
(*Headboard fitted*, *Fitted banquette*) ARE TGQ rows, and the fill-in screen
files them under "also". **Not measured on real data.**
